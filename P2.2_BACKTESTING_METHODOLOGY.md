# P2.2.2 — NIFTY Options Buying Backtesting Methodology

Date: 2026-11-06 · Baseline commit: `03a5d27` (clean) · Budget: ₹0 · Companion:
`P2.2_DATA_FEASIBILITY_REPORT.md`, `P2.2_OPTIONS_ADAPTATION_DESIGN.md`.

A **common, reproducible evaluation framework** for the five Tier-1 candidates
(**orders_12, orders_01, orders_18, orders_05, orders_07**). It specifies *how* to judge, not
*what wins*; no strategy is implemented or modified here. Every mechanism below maps to an
**existing** `nifty_quant/validation/*` API. Legend: **[FACT]** built/verified ·
**[ASSUMPTION]** declared choice · **[LIMITATION]** honest bound.

> Guiding rule: **all thresholds are fixed on Development data only. The out-of-sample
> holdout is evaluated once and can never be used to pick a threshold.** **[FACT]**
> `validation/splits.py::dev_oos_split` exists to enforce the chronology.

---

## 0. What a run consists of

Each candidate is run through the *identical* pipeline (adaptation design §1–4):

```
daily index signal (D)  ->  contract selection (type/strike/expiry, availability-gated)
  ->  OptionsExecutionEngine.run(scenario)  ->  IndiaOptionsCommission.round_trip  ->  per-trade record
```

A **per-trade record** carries: entry/exit date & premium, bars_held, dte_at_entry, exit reason,
gross_pnl, total_costs, net_pnl, `cost_basis`, `statutory_verified`, `all_in_verified`,
`strict_verifiable`, scenario name. **[FACT]** `TradeResult.as_dict()` / `OptionsCostResult`.

## 1. Chronological periods and the protected holdout

**[FACT]** Joint index+option data spans 2021-05-24 → 2026-02-24 (usable option days; the
2026-08 stub is excluded — feasibility §3). **[ASSUMPTION]** The eligible-trade calendar is
split **by date, chronologically, no shuffling, no leakage across the boundary**:

| Period | Date range (on the option-eligible timeline) | Purpose |
|--------|----------------------------------------------|---------|
| **Development** | 2021-05-24 → 2024-06-30 | Metric definitions, threshold-setting, diagnostics. |
| **Validation (walk-forward IS/OOS)** | 2024-07-01 → 2025-06-30 | Walk-forward folds; robustness; no threshold changes. |
| **Protected OOS holdout** | 2025-07-01 → 2026-02-24 | Computed **once**, at the end, thresholds frozen. |

* **[FACT]** Boundaries are `dev_oos_split(...)` inputs; the holdout is a single reserved tail.
* **[ADDED in P2.2.2.1]** Measured eligible-day load per period (index∩option days, all with
  matching index intraday): **Development 91, Validation 35, Holdout 32** (sum 158). After
  excluding expiry-day entries, ~**143** entry-opportunity days remain sample-wide.
* **[ADDED in P2.2.2.1]** Opportunity days ≠ trades: a `long_only` daily candidate books a trade
  only on a flat→long flip under a one-position/min-hold structure, so realistic per-candidate
  counts are **low double digits over the whole sample** and the **32-day holdout will very likely
  yield single-digit trades** — below the §3 floors. This is expected, not a reason to move a floor.
* **[LIMITATION]** Because the surface is only ~158 days, these ranges hold a **small** number
  of trades; the framework reports counts and intervals and expects "insufficient evidence"
  (§8) rather than inflating confidence.

## 2. Walk-forward evaluation

**[FACT]** Uses `validation/walkforward.py`:
* `generate_folds(...)` → chronological anchored/expanding **`Fold`**s (train window then the
  immediately following test window; test never overlaps the next train).
* `window_metrics(...)` computes per window over `_METRIC_KEYS = total_return, cagr, sharpe,
  sortino, max_drawdown, n_trades, win_rate, profit_factor, expectancy`.
* `walkforward_table(..., prefix_is="is_", prefix_oos="oos_")` and `fold_aggregate(...)` roll
  in-sample vs out-of-sample per fold; `walkforward_summary(..., min_trades=...)` marks folds
  below the trade floor as unreliable.

**[ASSUMPTION]** Fold geometry fixed a priori on Development: **train ≈ 6 eligible months, test
= the next 2 months, step 2 months, expanding from the first eligible day.** Chosen for
chronological integrity, **not** tuned to holdout outcome.

---

## 3. Trade-count requirement

**[FACT]** Reused floors: `walkforward_summary(min_trades=10)`, `consistency.symbol_consistency
(min_trades=5)`, `dev_oos_table(min_trades=3)`.

**[ASSUMPTION] Pre-registered screening floors** (fixed before seeing OOS):

| Gate | Floor | Consequence if unmet |
|------|------:|----------------------|
| Total trades (Development) | ≥ **30** | Candidate labelled *insufficient evidence*, not scored. |
| Trades per OOS fold | ≥ **5** | Fold reported but excluded from aggregates. |
| Exit-reason diversity | ≥ **2** distinct reasons | Flags a degenerate (single-forcing) rule. |

* **[LIMITATION]** On 158 days these floors are **likely unreachable**; the *correct* output is
  "insufficient evidence" — **never** a lowered floor to manufacture a pass.

---

## 4. Metrics — definitions (premium per-trade basis)

All in **₹ on the risked premium**, computed by `window_metrics` / `sensitivity.METRIC_KEYS`.

| Metric | Definition here |
|--------|-----------------|
| **net_pnl / trade** | `gross_pnl − total_costs` (must carry `cost_basis`; see §7). |
| **profit_factor** | `sum(win net) / abs(sum(loss net))` over trades; >1 required, but 1 is not "profitable" — it is break-even before the friction that follows. |
| **expectancy** | `mean(net_pnl)` per trade **and** per unit premium risked (`net_pnl / entry_premium·lot_size·lots`). |
| **win_rate** | share of trades with `net_pnl > 0`. |
| **max_drawdown** | peak-to-trough on the chronological cumulative `net_pnl` equity curve. |
| **sharpe / sortino** | on the per-trade return series (and daily-marked curve where bars allow); reported with CI (§6). |
| **cagr / total_return** | on the equity curve; **descriptive only** given short, non-contiguous windows. |

**[ASSUMPTION]** Positions valued at the last available close when `OPEN_AT_END`; `UNRESOLVED`
trades are **excluded from P&L** but **counted** in the unresolved tally (never given a
fabricated settlement value — execution model, adaptation §3).

## 5. Sensitivity to costs and slippage

**[FACT]** Two complementary mechanisms:
1. **Scenario spread** — every trade runs through `run_scenarios` over
   `OPTIMISTIC / BASELINE / CONSERVATIVE` (adverse 0 / 2 / 5 bps; CONSERVATIVE adds a liquidity
   floor). The spread *is* the fragility signal. **[FACT]** `OptionsExecutionEngine.run_scenarios`.
2. **Parameter perturbation** — `validation/sensitivity.py::run_sensitivity` / `perturb_grid`
   vary cost & execution knobs (STT/turnover rates, GST, slippage, TP/SL band, MIN_DTE),
   respecting `_breaks_constraints`; `plateau_summary(min_trades=...)` reports whether a result
   sits on a **plateau** (robust) or a **knife-edge** (fragile).

3. **[ADDED in P2.2.2.1] Resolution sensitivity** — repeat the primary 15m run on **5m** to
   confirm the result is not an artifact of bar size (26.0% of 1m bars are degenerate vs 6.7% at
   15m, so resolution matters). **[CORRECTED] The 0/2/5 bps slippage and the ±bps/liquidity knobs
   are *estimates*, not measured fills** (no bid/ask or tape exists), and the CONSERVATIVE
   `min_exit_volume=1.0` is only meaningful once re-expressed in share-equivalents as
   `lot_size×lots` (see adaptation design §3) — until then it is reported as a cosmetic gate.

**[ASSUMPTION] Qualification rule:** a candidate may only "advance" if it is
*break-even-or-better under **BASELINE*** and its Development-to-Validation sign does **not**
flip under CONSERVATIVE. Passing on OPTIMISTIC alone is explicitly treated as **fragile**.

## 6. Statistical robustness & multiple-testing safeguards

**[FACT]** The repo already ships the corrections; the design mandates them:

* **Confidence intervals** — `stats.bootstrap_mean_ci` on per-trade `net_pnl`, and
  `bootstrap_sharpe_ci` on the Sharpe estimate; report the CI, never the point alone.
* **Probabilistic Sharpe** — `stats.probabilistic_sharpe_ratio` (accounts for skew/kurtosis and
  sample length).
* **Deflated Sharpe / selection bias** — `stats.deflated_sharpe_ratio` with
  `n_trials = expected_max_sharpe(n_trials, sr_std)`'s trial count. **[ASSUMPTION]** `n_trials`
  is **pre-registered as the total number of distinct candidate×variant×scenario configurations
  evaluated** (the five candidates plus every perturbation run), so a single impressive number
  cannot survive cherry-picking.
* **False-discovery control across candidates** — `stats.benjamini_hochberg` over the five
  candidates' p-values; only FDR-surviving results are "significant".
* **Sign test** — `stats.binomial_sign_test(positive, negative)` as a distribution-free check
  that win/loss sign isn't luck at tiny n.

**[LIMITATION]** At n < 30 these tests have low power; non-significance is the *expected,
reported* outcome, not a failure to fix.

## 7. Cost-labelling discipline in reporting (ties to adaptation §4)

**[FACT] + rule:** every reported aggregate carries the cost tier:
* If `brokerage_excluded` and not `all_in_verified` (the current ₹0 default) → label
  **"statutory-only, brokerage-excluded"**; **never** "net profit".
* If any `estimated` statutory rate touched the trade (pre-Apr-2023 slab rows) →
  **"modelling only"**.
* Only when `all_in_verified=True` may a number be titled fully-all-in net.
* Headline P&L is always shown under **BASELINE** with the OPTIMISTIC↔CONSERVATIVE range.

## 8. Regime-based analysis

**[FACT]** `validation/regime.py` segments the evaluation by market state. **[ASSUMPTION]**
Regimes are defined from the **index series only** (no forward info): a trend/volatility
partition (e.g. realised-vol terciles and an SMA-slope up/down flag computed on prior data).
Per-regime `window_metrics` are reported.

**[LIMITATION]** With ~158 joint days the per-regime trade counts will often be < the floors in
§3; regime tables are therefore **descriptive**, not inferential, and are printed with counts so
the reader sees where a cell is empty.

## 9. Fair comparison — identical rules for all five

**[FACT]** The five candidates share, byte-for-byte, the same:

| Dimension | Common setting (from adaptation design) |
|-----------|------------------------------------------|
| Data eligibility | coverage-registry + `STRICT_OK` session + `available=True` bar + liquidity floor |
| Contract selection | type from signal; ATM±1 band, most-liquid strike; nearest eligible expiry ≥ MIN_DTE; availability-gated |
| Execution | `OptionsExecutionEngine` next-bar entry/exit, stop-wins, gap→worse, expiry/UNRESOLVED handling |
| Cost | `IndiaOptionsCommission`, effective-dated, brokerage-excluded by default, labelled |
| Metrics & gates | §3–§6 identical thresholds and tests |

Only the **signal definition** differs by design — that is the sole object of study.

**Baselines & references (included wherever data supports them):**
1. **[FACT] No-trade baseline** — zero positions, `net_pnl = 0`; the explicit bar to clear.
2. **[ASSUMPTION] Index buy-and-hold** — `^NSEI` / index 1m→daily close over the *same eligible
   dates*, as the directional yardstick (shows whether timing the option adds anything vs simply
   holding the index).
3. **[ASSUMPTION] Naive ATM long hold-to-expiry** — buy the ATM contract at D+1 open, hold to
   expiry, **no** stop/target — isolates the *option-selection + theta* cost from the candidate's
   exit cleverness; identical selection rules, only exits differ.
4. **[FACT] Cost-only baseline** — gross-vs-net gap on the same trade set, quantifying the
   statutory/brokerage-excluded friction.

**[LIMITATION]** Benchmarks are computed only on the joint-eligible calendar so they are
**like-for-like** with the candidates; they are references, not claims of profitability.

## 10. Decision rule (pre-registered; screening, not profit)

A candidate is assigned exactly one outcome, decided **without** touching holdout thresholds:

* **Advance to deeper study** — meets §3 trade floors on Development+Validation, break-even-or-
  better under **BASELINE**, sign-stable under CONSERVATIVE, survives §6 DSR/BH, and improves on
  the no-trade **and** naive-ATM baselines.
* **Insufficient evidence** — floors unmet or tests under-powered (the **expected** result on
  current data). Data acquisition (more expiries / a fuller eligible calendar) becomes the next
  step, not threshold relaxation.
* **Fails screening** — meets floors but loses to baselines / fragile across scenarios.

**[FACT]** This is *screening*, distinct from the trading gate. The live gate
(`signals/gating.py::DEFAULT_APPROVAL_CRITERIA`) approves only on validation **verdict**
(`supported`, `enough_trades`, `oos_profitable`, strict-split agreement) — **approval is never a
profitability assertion**, and a "fail"/"insufficient" here is not a claim that the strategy
loses money in reality.

## 11. Reproducibility contract

* **[FACT]** Fixed `ResearchConfig.seed = 20260101`; every result bundle persisted next to a
  config snapshot (`ResearchConfig.as_dict`) recording periods, scenario set, selection band,
  cost tier, `n_trials`, and the git commit.
* **[ASSUMPTION]** Deterministic selection rules (tie-breaks in adaptation §2) make the run
  re-runnable to the trade.
* **[LIMITATION]** Results are **modelling-grade** while `transaction_costs_verified` is
  fail-closed and option data covers ~158 days — the framework is built to be *correct on a tiny
  honest sample*, not to *manufacture a signal*.
